# Source Material Index

This note tracks the major sources that inform the VitalNotes rebuild.

The goal is not to cite these constantly in student-facing content.

The goal is to let them shape the thinking underneath the guide.

VitalNotes should feel practical, grounded, and student-facing. The sources should be used to improve judgment, structure, explanation, and transfer. They should not make the guide feel academic, performative, or over-referenced.

---

## Source Use Rule

Use these sources implicitly and practically.

Do not turn VitalNotes into a book report.

Do not name-drop unless there is a specific reason.

Do not make the student feel like they are reading academic commentary.

The student should feel like the material understands the problem and gives them a usable way forward.

## Teaching and Practice Sources

VitalNotes is grounded in the real learning problems seen in paramedic education.

Important teaching and practice sources include:

- student struggles observed in paramedic labs
- OSCE performance issues
- scenario day patterns
- common clinical reasoning errors
- feedback and GRS assessment experience
- scenario design principles
- Scenario Generator principles
- student hesitation around directives
- students knowing facts but struggling to connect them

These sources are especially important because VitalNotes is not only a learning science project.

## How to Use Source Material During Rebuild

When drafting or revising a section:

1. Confirm the section role in [[../02 Content Architecture/New VitalNotes Learning Path]].
2. Check related old material in [[../02 Content Architecture/Old-to-New Section Mapping]].
3. Use the book and learning science sources as background influence.
4. Preserve useful prior thinking without preserving old structure unnecessarily.
5. Keep the student problem explicit.
6. Keep the paramedic context explicit.
7. Use examples only when they clarify a decision, pattern, or learning problem.
8. Keep tools out unless the section earns a reusable process.
9. End by orienting toward the next section.
10. Update relevant architecture files after major cluster progress.

---

## What Not to Do With Sources

Do not:

- cite sources constantly in student-facing sections
- summarize books inside the guide
- turn learning science into academic explanation
- let source material override the student problem
- preserve old section structure just because it exists
- add every interesting idea
- make the guide feel like a literature review
- make the app expose internal source architecture
- let Obsidian become the visible student experience
