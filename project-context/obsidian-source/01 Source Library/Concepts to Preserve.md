# Concepts to Preserve

This note tracks core ideas that must survive the rebuild, even if the old section structure changes.

## Learning and Memory

- Review feels fluent but does not prove access.
- Retrieval strengthens access.
- Spacing helps learning survive delay and distraction.
- Difficulty during recall is useful information.
- Learning often feels worse before it becomes more reliable.

## Cognitive Load

- Overload is often structural, not personal.
- Working memory has limits.
- Structure protects thinking.
- Pressure narrows attention.
- Unnecessary load should be reduced so students can think where it matters.

## Notes and Knowledge Building

- Notes should support thinking, not just store information.
- Smart Notes should focus on one idea at a time.
- Notes should be written in the student's own words.
- Links should represent meaningful relationships.
- Notes mature through use, not polish.

## Meaning and Understanding

- Meaning is connection between ideas.
- Facts in isolation often fail under pressure.
- Mechanisms help students stay oriented before diagnosis is clear.
- Pathophysiology should explain patterns, not sit separately from practice.

## Directives

- Directives are guardrails for safe decision-making.
- Understanding intent reduces anxiety without loosening standards.
- Students should learn what a directive is protecting.
- Protocols do not replace judgment.

## Clinical Reasoning

- Reasoning is a moving explanation, not a final answer.
- Students can act safely before certainty.
- Reassessment tests whether the explanation still holds.
- Pattern recognition should propose hypotheses, not close the case.

## Practice and Performance

- Scenario days stress the learning system.
- OSCEs test thinking under evaluation pressure.
- Progress often appears as trajectory, not perfect performance.
- Recovery speed matters before consistency.

## Reflection

- Reflection should be brief, specific, and action-oriented.
- One moment and one adjustment is often enough.
- Rumination is not deeper learning.
- The Five Whys should find a learning leverage point.
