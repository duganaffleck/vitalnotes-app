# Teaching Observations

This note holds real observations from paramedic teaching, labs, scenarios, OSCEs, and student performance.

These observations should shape the rebuild more than abstract content categories.

## Common Student Struggles

- Students study heavily but struggle to use knowledge under pressure.
- Students often interpret overload as personal failure.
- Students know facts but struggle to connect them into clinical meaning.
- Students can perform steps but lose the reason behind them.
- Students hesitate when directives feel uncertain or high stakes.
- Students often wait for diagnostic certainty before acting safely.
- Students may lose reassessment after the first intervention.
- Students can appear polished while their reasoning remains fragile.
- Students may over-reflect after performance and turn learning into rumination.

## Scenario Day Observations

- Repeated scenarios reveal patterns better than single runs.
- The most useful feedback often identifies one adjustment, not every possible issue.
- Progress may show first as faster recovery, not perfect performance.
- Students need language for why errors happen under load.

## OSCE Observations

- Students often rush because they feel watched.
- Twelve minutes can feel short, but it is long enough to think if structure is protected.
- Students often need permission to pause briefly and reorient.
- Strong performance is not always smooth performance.
- Instructors can often tell the difference between confidence and defensible reasoning.

## How to Use This Note

When writing sections, return to this note and ask:

What real student problem is this section helping with?
