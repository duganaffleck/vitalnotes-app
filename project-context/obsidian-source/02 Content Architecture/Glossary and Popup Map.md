# Glossary and Popup Map

Glossary popups should support reading flow without turning the app into a textbook.

They should be short, plain-language, and student-facing. Their job is to help a student keep reading when a term might otherwise slow them down.

Popups should not carry the main teaching. If a concept is necessary to understand the section, it belongs in the main page. If a term appears repeatedly across the guide and needs a quick reminder, it may belong here.

## Popup Rules

- Keep definitions brief.
- Use paramedic relevance when useful.
- Avoid academic phrasing.
- Avoid citations in the popup itself.
- Do not define terms that students can understand from context.
- Do not overuse popups.
- Do not use popups to replace proper explanation in the section.
- Prefer plain language over technical precision when the plain version is enough.
- Add new terms only when they reduce friction across multiple sections.
- If a popup starts needing multiple paragraphs, the concept probably needs its own section or tool.

## Core Popup Terms

### Cognitive Load

Plain-language meaning:  
The amount of mental work your brain is trying to manage at one time.

Paramedic relevance:  
It rises quickly when assessment, communication, decision-making, memory, and procedures compete for attention.

### Working Memory

Plain-language meaning:  
The limited mental space used to hold and work with information right now.

Paramedic relevance:  
When it fills up, simple steps can disappear even when the student knows them.

### Overload

Plain-language meaning:  
When the amount of information, decisions, or tasks competing for attention becomes more than working memory can manage cleanly.

Paramedic relevance:  
Overload can make students miss simple steps, lose track of priorities, or fixate on one task even when they know the material.

### Structure

Plain-language meaning:  
A reliable pattern or routine that helps organize what to do next.

Paramedic relevance:  
Structure protects thinking during scenarios by reducing how many decisions students need to remake under pressure.

### Reassessment

Plain-language meaning:  
Checking whether the patient, your explanation, or your plan has changed after time, treatment, or new information.

Paramedic relevance:  
Reassessment helps students notice whether an intervention is working, whether the patient is worsening, or whether their first interpretation still fits.

### Retrieval

Plain-language meaning:  
Pulling information from memory without looking at the answer first.

Paramedic relevance:  
It matters because scenarios require access, not recognition.

### Recognition

Plain-language meaning:  
Knowing something when you see it again.

Paramedic relevance:  
Recognition can make studying feel productive, but scenarios require students to bring knowledge back without the answer sitting in front of them.

### Familiarity

Plain-language meaning:  
The feeling that material is known because you have seen it before.

Paramedic relevance:  
Familiarity can be reassuring during review, but it does not always mean the student can use the idea during a call, lab, or OSCE.

### Access

Plain-language meaning:  
Being able to bring knowledge back and use it when the cues are gone.

Paramedic relevance:  
Access matters in paramedicine because students need to retrieve and apply knowledge while assessing, communicating, deciding, and managing pressure.

### Spacing

Plain-language meaning:  
Returning to learning after time has passed instead of cramming everything into one block.

Paramedic relevance:  
It helps knowledge survive delay, distraction, and pressure.

### Clinical Recall

Plain-language meaning:  
Remembering information in a way that supports clinical use, not just answering a question.

Paramedic relevance:  
Clinical recall helps students bring back knowledge in a form that supports assessment, prioritization, directive use, reassessment, and communication.

### Recall Prompt

Plain-language meaning:  
A question or cue used to practice bringing knowledge back from memory.

Paramedic relevance:  
Better recall prompts help students notice something, decide something, avoid harm, reassess something, or explain their reasoning.

### Clinical Cue

Plain-language meaning:  
A finding, detail, symptom, trend, or piece of history that points attention toward a possible concern.

Paramedic relevance:  
Clinical cues help students connect what they are seeing or hearing to risk, pattern recognition, assessment priorities, and reassessment.

### Boundary

Plain-language meaning:  
A limit or guardrail that affects whether an action is safe, appropriate, allowed, or needs consultation.

Paramedic relevance:  
Boundaries help students remember when to act, withhold, stop, patch, reassess, or recognize something as outside scope.

### Trivia

Plain-language meaning:  
Isolated information remembered without much connection to use.

Paramedic relevance:  
Some facts matter, but recall becomes weak when students practice facts without connecting them to assessment, decisions, safety, or reassessment.

### Anki

Plain-language meaning:  
A flashcard app that uses spaced repetition to bring cards back for review over time.

Paramedic relevance:  
Anki can support retrieval and spacing, but it only helps clinical learning when the prompts are shaped around useful knowledge, not just disconnected facts.

### Flashcard

Plain-language meaning:  
A short question-and-answer study prompt.

Paramedic relevance:  
Flashcards can help with recall, but they need to be built carefully so students do not confuse answering cards with being ready for scenarios or patient care.

### Learning Strain

Plain-language meaning:  
The pressure or difficulty students feel when the demands of learning exceed the support, structure, or access they currently have.

Paramedic relevance:  
Learning strain can show up after labs, scenarios, OSCEs, studying, or feedback. It needs to be interpreted carefully instead of automatically treated as personal failure.

### Productive Difficulty

Plain-language meaning:  
Difficulty that strengthens learning because it forces recall, connection, adjustment, or more careful thinking.

Paramedic relevance:  
Productive difficulty helps students build knowledge that can be used under pressure, even if it feels less smooth at first.

### Wasted Difficulty

Plain-language meaning:  
Difficulty that consumes time and energy without improving understanding, recall, reasoning, or performance.

Paramedic relevance:  
Wasted difficulty can make students work harder without becoming steadier in labs, scenarios, or OSCEs.

### Meaning

Plain-language meaning:  
The way ideas connect to each other so they become useful, not just remembered.

Paramedic relevance:  
Meaning helps students understand what findings suggest together instead of treating each fact as separate.

### Schema

Plain-language meaning:  
A mental structure that groups related ideas so they can be recognized together.

Paramedic relevance:  
Schemas help students see patterns instead of juggling isolated facts.

### Pathophysiology

Plain-language meaning:  
How illness or injury changes the way the body is working.

Paramedic relevance:  
Pathophysiology helps students understand why signs and symptoms appear together and what might happen next.

### Mechanism

Plain-language meaning:  
The process causing a problem or pattern.

Paramedic relevance:  
Thinking in mechanisms helps students reason before they know the final diagnosis.

### Compensation

Plain-language meaning:  
The body’s attempt to maintain function when something is going wrong.

Paramedic relevance:  
Compensation can make a patient look stable before they deteriorate, so students need to watch trends and effort, not just single numbers.

### Perfusion

Plain-language meaning:  
Blood flow reaching the tissues well enough to support function.

Paramedic relevance:  
Poor perfusion can show up through skin signs, mental status changes, weak pulses, low blood pressure, delayed cap refill, or worsening overall appearance.

### Ventilation

Plain-language meaning:  
Moving air in and out of the lungs.

Paramedic relevance:  
Ventilation matters because a patient may have oxygen available but still be unable to move air effectively.

### Oxygen Delivery

Plain-language meaning:  
Getting oxygen from the air, into the blood, and out to the tissues.

Paramedic relevance:  
Oxygen delivery depends on breathing, circulation, hemoglobin, and tissue perfusion. It is broader than the SpO₂ number alone.

### Directive Intent

Plain-language meaning:  
What a directive is trying to protect, support, or prevent.

Paramedic relevance:  
Understanding intent helps students apply directives safely without waiting for perfect certainty.

### Clinical Risk

Plain-language meaning:  
The harm or deterioration that could happen if a problem is missed, delayed, or treated incorrectly.

Paramedic relevance:  
Clinical risk helps students decide what matters most when the diagnosis is not yet clear.

### Scope

Plain-language meaning:  
The care a paramedic is allowed and expected to provide within their role, training, and standards.

Paramedic relevance:  
Scope helps students understand the boundary between appropriate care, consultation, and actions that are not permitted.

### Contraindication

Plain-language meaning:  
A reason a treatment should not be given because it may cause harm.

Paramedic relevance:  
Contraindications help students apply directives safely instead of treating every eligible-looking patient the same way.

### Smart Notes

Plain-language meaning:  
Short notes written in your own words that help you explain, connect, and reuse one idea later.

Paramedic relevance:  
Smart Notes help students build understanding that can support reasoning during scenarios, OSCEs, and patient care instead of only storing information for review.

### Capture Notes

Plain-language meaning:  
Fast, rough notes used to catch an idea, question, cue, or observation before it disappears.

Paramedic relevance:  
Capture notes are useful during lectures, labs, scenarios, and debriefs because they preserve material that can be processed later.

### Working Notes

Plain-language meaning:  
Notes where you process raw material and begin turning it into your own understanding.

Paramedic relevance:  
Working notes help students wrestle with lecture content, readings, feedback, and scenario moments before turning them into more stable Smart Notes.

### Idea Maturation

Plain-language meaning:  
The way an idea becomes clearer, more connected, and more useful over time.

Paramedic relevance:  
Idea maturation helps students avoid treating early notes as final answers, especially when later scenarios or feedback change their understanding.

### Provisional Explanation

Plain-language meaning:  
A current best explanation that is useful for now but open to revision.

Paramedic relevance:  
Provisional explanations help students keep learning flexible when their understanding is still developing.

### Obsidian

Plain-language meaning:  
A note-making app that stores notes as plain text files and makes it easy to link ideas together.

Paramedic relevance:  
Obsidian can support Smart Notes, but it is only useful if it helps students think more clearly instead of becoming another system to manage.

### Vault

Plain-language meaning:  
The folder where Obsidian stores a group of notes.

Paramedic relevance:  
A vault can become a student’s learning workspace for notes, links, references, and developing explanations.

### Markdown

Plain-language meaning:  
A simple way of formatting plain text with headings, links, lists, and basic emphasis.

Paramedic relevance:  
Markdown keeps notes simple and durable without needing complex formatting.

### Links

Plain-language meaning:  
Connections between notes.

Paramedic relevance:  
Links help students connect physiology, directives, assessment findings, common errors, and scenario decisions.

### Inbox

Plain-language meaning:  
A temporary place for unprocessed notes, questions, and observations.

Paramedic relevance:  
An Inbox helps students capture important material quickly without stopping to organize it during class, lab, or debrief.

### Reference

Plain-language meaning:  
Material you may need to look up but do not need to rewrite as your own thinking.

Paramedic relevance:  
Reference material can include directive text, tables, PDFs, or lecture slides. It supports learning but should not replace explanation.

### Clinical Reasoning

Plain-language meaning:  
Keeping a workable explanation of what is happening and adjusting it as new information appears.

Paramedic relevance:  
It is less about naming the final diagnosis and more about managing risk as the call changes.

### Pattern Recognition

Plain-language meaning:  
Recognizing a familiar constellation of findings.

Paramedic relevance:  
It can speed thinking, but it must stay accountable to reassessment and disconfirming cues.

### Premature Closure

Plain-language meaning:  
Settling too early on an explanation and ignoring information that does not fit.

Paramedic relevance:  
It can make a student look confident while their thinking becomes less safe.

### Metacognition

Plain-language meaning:  
Noticing and adjusting how you are thinking.

Paramedic relevance:  
It helps students catch fixation, hesitation, and overload earlier.

### Reflection

Plain-language meaning:  
Looking back at one meaningful moment so it can change what you notice or do next time.

Paramedic relevance:  
Reflection is useful when it leads to a specific adjustment. It becomes less useful when it turns into replaying the whole call.

### Scenario Days

Plain-language meaning:  
Practice days where repeated scenarios expose patterns in thinking, assessment, communication, and decision-making.

Paramedic relevance:  
Scenario days are not just tests of what students know. They show how learning behaves when it has to be used repeatedly.

### OSCE Preparation

Plain-language meaning:  
Preparing for an observed clinical station by protecting your thinking, structure, and decision-making under evaluation pressure.

Paramedic relevance:  
OSCE preparation is not just memorizing what to do. It is learning how to keep assessment, reasoning, communication, and reassessment available while being watched and timed.

### Performance Under Pressure

Plain-language meaning:  
How thinking, memory, habits, and decisions behave when stress, time, evaluation, or uncertainty are present.

Paramedic relevance:  
Students often know more than they can access under pressure. The goal is to keep thinking usable when the situation feels heavier.

### Learning Path

Plain-language meaning:  
The suggested order for moving through the guide.

Paramedic relevance:  
It gives students a structured way through the material, while still allowing them to start with the problem they are actually having.

### Tools Library

Plain-language meaning:  
The place where reusable VitalNotes templates, checks, and short workflows are collected.

Paramedic relevance:  
The Tools Library helps students return to practical supports without rereading full sections every time.

## Possible Future Popup Terms

These terms may be added if they appear often enough in rebuilt sections.

### Spaced Repetition

Possible meaning:  
A review pattern where material returns over time so memory is practiced after some forgetting has happened.

Reason to track:  
Useful if the Anki or app content needs to distinguish the general idea of spacing from software-driven spaced repetition.

### Disconfirming Cues

Possible meaning:  
Findings that do not fit your current explanation.

Reason to track:  
Useful for pattern recognition, premature closure, and clinical reasoning sections.

### Generative Learning

Possible meaning:  
Learning by explaining, connecting, or reconstructing ideas in your own words.

Reason to track:  
May appear in Smart Notes, reflection, and retrieval sections, but may not need to be named directly.

### Deliberate Practice

Possible meaning:  
Focused practice aimed at improving one specific weakness rather than simply repeating the whole task.

Reason to track:  
Likely useful in scenario days, feedback, OSCE preparation, and practice improvement sections.

### Transfer

Possible meaning:  
The ability to use learning in a new situation, not just recognize it where it was first studied.

Reason to track:  
Important across the whole guide, especially because VitalNotes is concerned with learning that carries into scenarios and patient care.

### Problem-Based Entry Point

Possible meaning:  
A way into the guide based on what the student is currently struggling with rather than the formal section order.

Reason to track:  
Useful for the app interface, but may remain internal unless students see this phrase directly.
